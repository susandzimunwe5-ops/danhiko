About Danhiko Industrial Training College

A prominent technical and vocational institution in Harare, pioneering inclusive skills acquisition and vocational rehabilitation under the Danhiko Project welfare organization.

About the College

Danhiko Industrial Training College is a technical and vocational institution focused on providing practical skills, technical education and inclusive training opportunities. Operating under the broader Danhiko Project welfare organization, the college is recognized for its dedicated commitment to inclusive education, skills training, rehabilitation, and vocational development—particularly supporting students with disabilities and economically disadvantaged individuals.

Heritage-Based Education 5.0

The college emphasizes Heritage-Based Education 5.0, combining practical psychomotor capabilities with technical research and innovation to address real-world industry problems and bolster sustainable national development.

Through hands-on workshops and industry-aligned syllabi, Danhiko bridges the gap between foundational technical acumen and active economic empowerment, ensuring no student is left behind regardless of physical ability.

Inclusive Education & Accessibility

Promoting an inclusive learning environment where individuals with and without physical impairments learn together as peers.

Disability Mainstreaming & Integrated Vocational Training

At Danhiko, diversity is the foundation of institutional growth. We believe in complete disability mainstreaming: students with and without physical impairments share identical workshops, laboratory facilities, and lecture halls, breaking down societal stigma and fostering mutual respect.

Student Health, Awareness & Welfare Initiatives

Danhiko Industrial Training College actively prioritizes comprehensive student welfare programs. The college has participated in partnerships such as UNESCO's O3 PLUS, supporting student welfare and awareness initiatives, including sexual and reproductive health awareness and advocacy tailored toward youths with disabilities.
